## Using

**<a href="https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.2" target="_blank">GLM-5.2</a>**: Finally. An open-weights model that can compete with the big boys. For the past weeks I've been using GLM-5.2 over almost every other model. I'm using it through [OpenCode Go](https://opencode.ai/go), which starts at 5 USD/month. It's different than using your traditional GPT 5.5, or Fable, etc. But it feels good that OpenSource is catching up. And I definetly urge you to take it for a spin. It does cost 1/4th of any other frontier alternative.

**<a href="https://github.com/phun333/pi-infobar" target="_blank">Pi Stats</a>**: You probably know by now. That I'm a big fan of the [Pi](https://pi.dev/) coding agent. You like Claude Code better, that's fine - enjoy yourself. For Pi - and many other coding agents out there - it's hard to know exactly how much your spending. I discovered this little application called PiStats, which allows me to have a widget in my menu bar that shows me exactly how much I've spend and in what. And even though I still pay for a fixed plan every month - it's still interesting to know (in terms of real value) what I'm spending.

**<a href="https://github.com/jesseduffield/lazygit" target="_blank">Lazygit</a>**: We write less code. But produce much more code nowadays. Some of it it's not worth looking at. The important bits are. For the past months I've experimented with a lot of "code diff viewers". Somewhere I can review the the changes the agent has made, and direct it in case anything goes wrong/weird. I've tried [Hunk](https://github.com/modem-dev/hunk) (and even contributed a couple of PRs: [#310](https://github.com/modem-dev/hunk/pull/310), [#347](https://github.com/modem-dev/hunk/pull/347)), and [codediff.nvim](https://github.com/esmuellert/codediff.nvim) in Neovim, which is what my `<space>d+d` mapping calls. But lazygit paired with [difftastic](https://github.com/Wilfred/difftastic) has been hard to beat. Fast to iterate, great diff highlighting.

**<a href="https://www.sofascore.com/" target="_blank">Sofascore</a>**: I like watching sports. Not all sports, not all the time. But when I do (surpirse) I like looking at the data. Sofascore is the app I've been using for many years now that gives me the data I need about the match I'm watching. Who plays where? Who used to play where? Those questions, and many more. This team out of Crotia has build an incredible app!


## Reading

**<a href="https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/deep-learning-for/9781098168025/" target="_blank">Deep Learning for Biology</a>**: Finished this one. Over 2-3 weeks. An interesting introduction to the area that intersects bio with deep learning. Definelty a lot of inspiration for what is possible and what there is left to do! 

**<a href="https://medarc-ai.github.io/mindeye/" target="_blank">Reconstructing the Mind's Eye</a>**: Some nuts research out of Princeton. That show how they reconstruct images from brain activity. Yes. You heard that right. 

**<a href="https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-06-24-scaling-laws/" target="_blank">Scaling Laws, Carefully - Lilian Weng</a>**: What is the ideal amount of data given a certain model size? Given a certain compute budget? And vice versa? These have been just some of the questions researchers have been asking themselves. Lilian writes about them beautifully.

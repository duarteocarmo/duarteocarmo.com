July was busy — like always — but we made sure to take some time and touch some grass. Or mountains in our case. With the high temperatures across Europe we went to the Trentino region and hike close to the Dolomites for 5 days. Cool temperatures and beautiful scenery. Vitto's idea  of course.

Work is busy - but very fun as always. Allegra is growing fast which is incredible to see (while trying to fight sleep debt as much as we can).

## Using

**<a href="https://herdr.dev/" target="_blank">herdr</a>**: Never thought I'd see the day where something would replace Tmux for me. Tmux is *ingrained* in my muscle memory (a bit like Vim). Tmux is just the way I've always worked. When my friend <a href="https://x.com/strickvl" target="_blank">Alex</a> asked me if I was already in the herdr train, I told him no, of course - why should I? Until I realised I used multiple agents, but always had to keep checking if they were done. Not anymore. Oh, and the tmux-like bindings also help. 

**<a href="https://www.outdoorsupply.co.uk/child-carrier-bergans-kids-lilletind-grey" target="_blank">Bergans Kids Lilletind Child Carrier</a>**: There's no way we could've done 50 KM+ of hiking with an 8 month old without this thing. Vitto bought it used in Denmark and Allegra loved it.

**<a href="https://github.com/ggml-org/Llama-macOS" target="_blank">Llama macOS</a>**: Local LLMs are here to stay. I'm convinced we won't need frontier intelligence (e.g., Sol or Fable) for every single thing under the sun. I want my experience when running local LLMs to be simple, but powerful. 

**<a href="https://organicmaps.app" target="_blank">Organic Maps</a>**: I use a Garmin watch. But I hate the Garmin Connect app: slow, buggy, and just too much. You want to follow a trail, you have a GPX file for it, and just want to follow it: Garmin Connect complicates things, Organic Maps doesn't. 

**<a href="https://docs.rsshub.app/routes/youtube" target="_blank">RSSHub</a>**: RSS is how I read news. I follow hundreds of websites on my [self-hosted FreshRSS]({filename}/posts/84-drowing-in-news.md) and use <a href="https://netnewswire.com" target="_blank">NetNewsWire</a> to read them. Everyone is (still) actively trying to force you to go to their website even though I don't want to. I want to use RSS. For Reddit, I found a solution: I use <a href="https://github.com/johnwarne/reddit-top-rss" target="_blank">toprss</a>. Now, with RSSHub I can do the same for Youtube channels. I like having all the things in the same place. 

**<a href="https://github.com/manalkaff/opendesign" target="_blank">OpenDesign</a>**: You like Claude Design, I like Claude Design, but I don't like having an Anthropic subscription. OpenDesign solves just that. It's a Claude Design inspired product, but it's (1) open-source, and (2) you can use it with any coding agent/model under the sun. Your vibe coded presentation won't be pristine - but it will get you 80% of the way there, and allow you to iterate on a bunch of potential good designs.

**<a href="https://austinkleon.com/show-your-work/" target="_blank">Show Your Work!</a>**: A nice read for those who like doing creative work. How to build an audience, why having your website is important. Not life changing - but light, well written, and inspiring. 

**<a href="https://poolside.ai/assets/laguna/laguna-m1-xs2-technical-report.pdf" target="_blank">Laguna M.1/XS.2 Technical Report</a>**: One of the most well-written technical reports I've read in a while. Doesn't tell you *all* the secrets, but gives you enough detail to be really educational. Software, pre-training, SFT, infrastructure, RL, what went *wrong* during training.

**<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00037" target="_blank">Moshi: A speech-text foundation model for real-time dialogue</a>**: How do you build a full-duplex model? Something that can speak and listen just like we do? Moshi is an *oldish* paper but goes into exactly that. Also recommend the <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.08753" target="_blank">DSM paper</a> from the same lab.

**<a href="https://www.manning.com/books/build-a-reasoning-model-from-scratch" target="_blank">How to Build a Reasoning Model from Scratch</a>**: I've read almost all of Raschka's books. I love the way he writes. Onto his latest.



Ok. Now we are getting dangersouly close to the end of the year. 'Legra will soon be a full year old. She stands, has teeth, and is close to walking. Slowly but steadily the houselhold is getting into some sort of chaotic, and highly unpredictable routine.

I went to Lisbon for a couple of days and spoke at Lisbon AI. Vitto and I are now back to running together thanks to a great second-hand purchase.

Didn't really have much time for reading this month so I stuck with mostly papers and articles. Lots of interesting podcasts and other tidbits though.


## Using

**<a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20151208002047/http://www.babyjogger.com/product/fit/" target="_blank">Baby Jogger F.I.T.</a>**: I never wanted to spend 500 EUR. on a running stroller withouth knowing if Allegra would like it. I've heard enumerous stories of babies that hate going for runs. So I found this one for ~80 EUR. I think this might be one of the best purchases we've made as parents. Allegra _tolerates_ it. We've gonne for multiple 10K + runs with it. And now we can go the three of us!

**<a href="https://muesli.works/" target="_blank">Muesli</a>**: We are all talking to our computers now. I've tested most of these apps. Superwhisper, handy, macwhisper. They all have pros and cons. Muelsi has no cons. It's local. It's fast. It's customizable. It records mettings, it does Speech-to-text. It's free, Open Source, and more importantly: 100% local. 

**<a href="https://www.moergo.com/pages/go60" target="_blank">MoErgo Go60</a>**: I know I have a couple of reviews on my backlog for this blog. This is one of them. For now, all I can say is that the last time I felt I liked a keyboard so much was when I bought the Happy Hacking Keyboard. This one is different. This one takes more getting used to. But slowly it's becoming my daily driver. And it's also extremely portable.


## Reading

**<a href="https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/vision-language-models/9798341624047/" target="_blank">Vision Language Models: Building VLMs with Hugging Face</a>**: So many good names in the industry getting together to write a book can only be good news. Started this one this month. 

**<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-84925-6" target="_blank">The Machinery of Life</a> by David S. Goodsell**: Beautiful but extremely dense. It's not the longest so I'm trying to plow through this one. 

Here should be the new xiami mimo report: Read this one on the flight to Lisbon. A very well written technical report that gets into the nitty gritty. The RL part and distillation are the most interesting parts of the report. MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B is my main daily local driver. 

**<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11690" target="_blank">Beyond Per-Token Pricing: A Concurrency-Aware Methodology for LLM Infrastructure Cost Estimation</a>**: Self-hosting LLMs on your hardware is difficult math. You can't just look at VRAM and token throughput, you need to get familiar with the use case. If you don't: you'd be better off with a cloud model. 

**<a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/24/opinion/congress-ai-sam-altman.html" target="_blank">Dear Congress: Here's What You Should Ask A.I. Leaders</a> (The New York Times)**: I tend to often agree with Cal. I don't like all this fear mongering going around from Anthropic and OpenAI. In this op-ed, Cal asks the important questions. Recommended reading even if you don't work in the area.
